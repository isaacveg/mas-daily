#### <span data-idx="1"></span>ODSA: Orchestrated Discovery and Self-Aggregation

> **ODSA：编排式发现与自聚合**

- **arXiv**：[2611.11621](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11621) ｜ **PDF**：[下载](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.11621) ｜ **HTML**：[全文](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.11621v1)
- 机构（T0）：Columbia University；KTH Royal Institute of Technology ｜ 主要作者：Macardle Meng（共 2 人）
- **arXiv 分类**：Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI) ; Computation and Language (cs.CL); Machine Learning (cs.LG); Multiagent Systems (cs.MA)
- **摘要（中文·Agent翻译）**：测试时扩展通过在推理阶段投入更多计算来提升语言模型回答的准确率。现有方法或在串行意义上如此，即经多轮迭代修改单一解法；或在并行意义上如此，即生成若干相互独立的解法并从中择优。我们提出 ODSA（Orchestrated Discovery and Self-Aggregation，编排式发现与自聚合），这是一个由大语言模型主导的扩展框架：编排器大语言模型把已有的测试时扩展方法作为可执行动作提供给它，这些动作包括发现新的独立解法、比较现有解法以构造更优解法，以及接受某个候选答案（此动作会触发对该答案的验证），并指挥无状态工作器执行这些动作。编排器本身并不求解问题：它负责分配计算资源，把先前动作的结果注入后续动作，并且始终在同一个上下文中做出每一个动作决策，从而使每次决策都能参考此前尝试过的一切。在 Humanity's Last Exam 上，相较未经任何测试时扩展的单次直接调用模型，ODSA 在三个模型上分别将准确率提升了 4.7 至 18.0 个点。在 LiveCodeBench 上，尽管单次直接调用已能解决大多数问题，ODSA 仍将准确率提升了 0.6 至 14.0 个点。

<details>
<summary>摘要（英文原文）</summary>

Test-time scaling improves the accuracy of a language model's answers by spending more computation at inference time. Established methods do so either sequentially, revising a single solution over several passes, or in parallel, generating several independent solutions and selecting among them. We present ODSA (Orchestrated Discovery and Self-Aggregation), an LLM-directed scaling framework in which an orchestrator LLM is given existing test-time scaling methods as actions --- discovery of a new independent solution, comparison of existing solutions to build a better one, and acceptance of a proposed answer, which triggers its verification --- and directs stateless workers to carry them out. The orchestrator does not solve the problem itself: it allocates computation, feeds the results of earlier actions into later ones, and takes every action from a single context, so that each decision is informed by everything tried before. On Humanity's Last Exam, ODSA improves accuracy by 4.7 to 18.0 points across three models over a single direct call to the model with no test-time scaling. On LiveCodeBench, where a single direct call already solves most problems, it improves accuracy by 0.6 to 14.0 points.

<summary>摘要（英文原文）</summary>

Low-bit quantization reduces the inference cost of LLM agents but can compromise task success. Examining failed rollouts, we find that quantized agents overlook task requirements or environmental conditions, producing environment transitions that diverge from successful full-precision references. Correcting these behavioral divergences remains difficult: offline data may miss the student's failure contexts, while its own rollouts may provide few correct responses in those contexts. We introduce SteerDistill, a quantization-aware distillation method in which a full-precision teacher guides the student to revise failed responses and iteratively refines its guidance using feedback from student execution. This closed-loop teacher steering produces locally verified behavioral corrections. We internalize these corrections by training the student without guidance to match the steered student's predictions at corrected turns and the full-precision teacher's at other turns, enabling unassisted inference. Across five agentic benchmarks and three models, SteerDistill exceeds the strongest quantized baseline by 1.67--2.18 points in average score under W4A16 and stays within one point of BF16. On $\tau^2$-Bench, it outperforms QeRL by 1.84 percentage points in success rate using 67% fewer training GPU hours and produces shorter successful trajectories than the quantized baselines.
