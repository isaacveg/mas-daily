#### <span data-idx="1"></span>EPSBench: Benchmarking LLM Agents for Earnings Forecasting

- **arXiv**：[2610.38293](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38293) ｜ **PDF**：[下载](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.38293) ｜ **HTML**：[全文](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.38293v1)
- 机构（T1）：Georgia Institute of Technology ｜ 主要作者：Yanbin Yin（共 5 人）
- **arXiv 分类**：Computation and Language (cs.CL) ; Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)

<details>
<summary>摘要（英文原文）</summary>

Quarterly earnings forecasting requires an agent to connect financial evidence to the income-statement line items that produce a company's reported earnings. We introduce EPSBench, a benchmark for studying this ability across 149 firm-quarters from 86 US companies. Agents read a fixed collection of filings, earnings-call transcripts, and news, then estimate company-specific income-statement drivers with cited evidence. A shared accounting calculator converts these estimates into GAAP diluted earnings per share (EPS), providing a common basis for comparing forecast assumptions. We evaluate six models under a common harness, assessing point forecast error relative to date-matched analyst consensus and checking whether their prediction intervals achieve the stated coverage. Three models show no detectable paired difference in point forecast error from consensus, and three have higher error. Across all six models, nominal 80% intervals contain reported EPS in only 37--70% of runs that return an interval, and most misses fall above the upper bound because the forecasts run low. The driver records trace most of the error gap between models to operating-income estimates.

#### <span data-idx="3"></span>Learning to Formulate: Agentic Hardware-Software Co-Design of AI Infrastructure through Progressive Optimization and Verification

- **arXiv**：[2610.39380](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39380) ｜ **PDF**：[下载](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39380) ｜ **HTML**：[全文](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.39380v1)
- 机构（T1）：National University of Singapore ｜ 主要作者：Yi Ding（共 2 人）
- **arXiv 分类**：Hardware Architecture (cs.AR) ; Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI); Distributed, Parallel, and Cluster Computing (cs.DC)

<details>
<summary>摘要（英文原文）</summary>

AI infrastructure, the chips and software that train and serve AI models, is designed in layers. Layers make the system manageable, but they also fix which choices an optimizer can consider. Gains that need the chip and its software to change together are excluded before optimization begins: the formulation leaves them out. Language-model agents make it possible to revisit these boundaries, but an agent's proposal cannot show by itself that a design is correct or optimal. We present Formulaic, a flow that divides the work. The agent writes the formulation, a mathematical solver optimizes within it, and verification and measurement check its assumptions and results. The agent refines the problem step by step, from the computation graph toward the chip. Each step is a template: a family of programs with the conditions under which they are correct. Wall relaxation searches the chip by pricing the hardware limits that bind the software. This paper takes a first step, through separate studies rather than one end-to-end run. On NVIDIA GB10, templates the agent wrote run complete Qwen3-8B layers $1.075\times$ faster than SGLang. In a simulated Ventus GPU study, joint design cuts the worst-scenario cycles of Qwen2.5-0.5B to 0.428 of the reference, against 0.934 for software alone. On a five-step GEMM refinement chain, reusing proofs makes checking $2.51\times$ faster.

#### <span data-idx="5"></span>What Does an Ontology Catch? Curated, LLM-Generated and Data-Derived Ontologies as Checkers of LLM Agents over Knowledge Graphs

- **arXiv**：[2610.37809](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37809) ｜ **PDF**：[下载](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.37809) ｜ **HTML**：[全文](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.37809v1)
- 机构（T1）：AGH University of Krakow ｜ 主要作者：Michal Orzechowski（共 3 人）
- **arXiv 分类**：Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI) ; Databases (cs.DB)
- **作者备注**：13 pages, 2 figures. Under review

<details>
<summary>摘要（英文原文）</summary>

LLM agents that answer questions over Web knowledge graphs are increasingly wrapped in checks derived from the graph's ontology, on the assumption that a formal schema makes a reliable judge. Yet a checker can be wrong as well: it can miss errors, and it can flag correct answers and push the agent into "fixing" them. Whether a curated ontology is worth its cost as such a judge, and how it compares with an ontology an LLM writes or a schema mined from the data, is unknown. We make the checker itself the object of study. Across curated, LLM-generated and data-derived ontologies, used as prompt context, as a checker, or both, and across open-weight and proprietary agents on public graphs and a private graph of nearly $10^{9}$ triples, we account for what ontology-derived checks catch and what they wrongly flag. We show how each source trades caught errors against false alarms, how a curated schema used as published can contradict its own data, which errors structural checks miss, and whether combining sources or giving the agent the checker's specific feedback pays off. On UniProt, the curated ontology flags more correct answers than either an ontology derived from the data or one an LLM derives from a summary of the data with masked identifiers. On real agent answers, its flags are only marginally more precise than random flags.

#### <span data-idx="6"></span>Sandman: Building and Operating Sandboxes for Agentic Reinforcement Learning on Shared Kubernetes

- **arXiv**：[2610.39711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39711) ｜ **PDF**：[下载](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39711) ｜ **HTML**：[全文](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.39711v1)
- 机构（T1）：Amazon ｜ 主要作者：Yiming Yan（共 15 人）
- **arXiv 分类**：Distributed, Parallel, and Cluster Computing (cs.DC) ; Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI); Machine Learning (cs.LG)
- **作者备注**：42 pages, 6 figures, 53 tables

<details>
<summary>摘要（英文原文）</summary>

Agentic reinforcement learning (RL) often requires repeated tool calls in task-specific environments. A training job may create thousands of these environments in bursts while sharing a cluster with other jobs. We present Sandman, a sandbox service built and operated at Amazon for agent tool execution and evaluation. It serves multiple production agentic RL workloads on shared Kubernetes. A training framework supplies a task image and uses commands and file operations in the returned sandbox. Files and processes persist across calls until the framework releases the environment. Sandman manages creation, readiness and cleanup. Each sandbox is a Kubernetes pod with a small daemon mounted into the unmodified image. In a scale test, Sandman held 11,858 concurrent tiny sandboxes. Four production SWE-bench Verified training campaigns created 3.1 million sandboxes in October 2026. We report the design trade-offs and operational lessons from handling creation bursts, preparing task environments, reclaiming abandoned sandboxes, and running evaluation under controlled conditions. We will open-source Sandman's daemon, provider and pool.

#### <span data-idx="7"></span>From extreme weather to agricultural consequences with agentic AI

- **arXiv**：[2610.40641](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40641) ｜ **PDF**：[下载](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40641) ｜ **HTML**：[全文](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.40641v1)
- 机构（T1）：University of Maryland；Stony Brook University ｜ 主要作者：Minghan Yu（共 4 人）
- **arXiv 分类**：Atmospheric and Oceanic Physics (physics.ao-ph) ; Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)
- **作者备注**：34 pages, 4 figures, 6 tables

<details>
<summary>摘要（英文原文）</summary>

Extreme weather is forecast days ahead, yet its consequences for agriculture remain hard to predict: a forecast says where a storm will strike, not which crops will suffer or how much is at risk, and the records that could tell span many sources. We present WxCrop, the first agent that judges agricultural hazards from physically interpretable weather indices examined by causal analysis, and the first that combines weather and agricultural information to predict crop loss reliably for past and forecast dates. We demonstrate it in Florida on hurricanes, freezes and dry heat, with a loss model calibrated on insurance records for each, and the same procedure extends to other hazards and areas. Three checks show reliability: the hurricane index detects recorded cyclone days with an AUC of 0.98, recorded losses in held-out years are reproduced to within 2%, and independent production records confirm the county rankings (rank correlation 0.63). Users ask the agent for each crop group's loss as a fraction of expected season value, every number sourced, so that governments, growers and scientists respond to agricultural disasters quickly.

</details>
